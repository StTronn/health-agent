# Common complaints

Each entry: what to ask, home care, when to see a doctor. Emergencies are in red-flags.md; lower thresholds in risk.md.

## Fever
- **Ask:** how long, highest reading (note °C vs °F), drinking and wees, alertness, rash, neck, breathing, other symptoms.
- **Home care:** fluids, rest, light clothing; fever medicine only if uncomfortable (medicines.md).
- **Doctor if:** fever for 3 days or more, or rising again after improving; a new symptom suggesting infection (ear pain, painful peeing, cough with fast breathing).
- **Usually fine:** a well, alert child who is drinking, with a fever under 3 days.

## Cough and cold
- **Ask:** how long, breathing (fast, noisy, ribs pulling in), fever, drinking, sleep.
- **Home care:** fluids, rest, saline drops for a blocked nose, honey for cough over age 1.
- **Doctor if:** cough beyond 3 weeks, fever beyond 3 days, wheeze, or getting worse after a week.

## Vomiting and diarrhoea
- **Ask:** how often, how long, keeping fluids down, wees, blood, green vomit, tummy pain, alertness.
- **Home care:** small frequent sips of oral rehydration solution, then normal food as tolerated.
- **Doctor if:** can't keep fluids down for 8 hours, vomiting beyond 2 days, diarrhoea beyond 7 days, or signs of dehydration (few wees, dry mouth, no tears).

## Sore throat
- **Ask:** how long, fever, swallowing, drooling, voice, breathing.
- **Home care:** warm fluids, salt-water gargles (older children and adults), paracetamol or ibuprofen if suitable.
- **Doctor if:** lasting beyond a week, high fever, or very painful swallowing.

## Headache
- **Ask:** how long, how bad, where, fever, neck stiffness, light sensitivity, vomiting, any head injury.
- **Home care:** rest, fluids, a dark quiet room, paracetamol if suitable.
- **Doctor if:** frequent or worsening headaches, or headache with fever that isn't settling.

## Not a reason to escalate
A missing detail you can ask about or check (a dose, a syrup's strength, an exact time), or a question with a clear safe answer ("no, don't use leftover antibiotics"), is not by itself a reason to see a doctor.
